## 2. How the system works

One Railway container runs the FastAPI server, the scheduler (every 10 minutes) and all workers.
State is JSON files on a 49 GB volume plus a small SQLite file. The site (Next.js on Vercel) proxies
to it.

| Stage | Input → output | Model / service | Checks that exist | Retry / recovery | Person involved | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source monitoring | series pages → chapter list, release dates, first-seen time | HTTP scrape | status per mirror | every 6 h | adds series | Working; **stopped whenever autopilot was off** (fixed on branch) |
| Chapter detection | watchlist → "next chapter" per series | — | paywall wait (24 h Asura) | — | — | Working |
| Ingest: scrape | chapter URL → page images | HTTP | short-chapter warning | none; no timeout | — | Working; re-runs on every pass |
| Split | pages → panel crops | geometry + YOLO; ≤60 vision checks (3.5-flash) | fallback to legacy splitter | re-runs on every pass, uncached | — | Working |
| Describe | crop → text, description, speaker | gemini-3.8-flash, 1 call per panel | per-panel cache by image + prompt | 4 attempts | — | Working; cache blocked chapters after any deploy (fixed on branch) |
| Script | descriptions → narration | 3.8-flash: beat sheet, ~9 units, critique, ≤3 rewrites | critique; chatbot-phrase guard | 3 attempts; no checkpoint | — | Working; rewrites never re-checked (fixed on branch) |
| Voice | lines → mp3 | gemini-3.8-flash-tts, 1 call per line | content-hash cache | 5 attempts | — | Working; a failed line could brick the chapter (fixed on branch) |
| Match / timeline | lines → panels, timing | embeddings; DP aligner, order kept | validator rules (advisory) | — | board edits | Working |
| Crops / framing | shot → crop box | 2.5-flash crop planner; 3.8-flash subject box | crop gate | failures fall back to full panel | — | **Crop planner broken in production since Nov 8** (fixed on branch) |

| Packaging | script → title, description, tags, thumbnail | 3.5/3.8-flash; local thumbnail | limits, duplicate title | every 6 h | edits, approves | Working; not versioned |
| Scheduling | queue → slot | Posting Review sheet gate | approved, unchanged, metadata valid | missed slot moves | fills the sheet | Working |
| Publishing | export → YouTube | Upload-Post | request status | 3 attempts; **no recovery after a restart** (fixed on branch) | — | Working |

### Found, not fixed (ranked)

1. **Release rule conflict** (owner decision): the Nov 8 rule (merges never overlap posted videos, 7 new chapters each) yields about one release every two days from 23 weekly series; the cumulative model in the brief yields about three a day. See section 6.
2. Manual merges ignore chapters already posted or uploading (`_range_ctx`), so a chapter can post alone and inside a merge. Fix depends on decision 1.
3. `_propagate_series_defaults` rewrites title, description and targets of queued and posted videos; with several channels it would re-route queued videos.
4. Multi-channel is partial: per-video targets exist, but schedule, sheet tab, title template, brand constants, voice and the Upload-Post profile are global. No channel registry.
5. Only one chapter is made at a time (one ingest worker, one render lock); module-level state in the scraper, narrator and renderer prevents more.
6. A paused chapter holds the only worker.
7. Speaker labels are unstable: two runs at the same setting disagreed on 10 of 24 panels.
8. 8 of 40 render-queue items failed with "error while opening encoder"; one chapter was re-added 4 times.
9. Approvals record no reviewer; the edit log is deleted on re-run; review time is not recorded.
10. Renders run inside the web process.
11. Export retention (7 days) runs only from a page the new site never opens; trash and leftover sweeps are not on the scheduler.
12. Thumbnail badge range is painted over the previous one instead of drawn from the stored range (branch `fix/merge-thumb-badge` exists, unpushed).

### Results

- **Script, 59 chapters:** 93 editor flags (1.58 a chapter): 61 invented facts, 18 style, 8 spoiled reveals, 5 missed beats, 1 redundancy. 53 chapters had at least one. 91 units were rewritten; none was re-checked. This is a model's review, not a human-verified error rate.
- **Media, 96 exports:** 66 pass (68.8%). All 30 failures are merged videos with timestamp faults at joins.
- **Timeline, 59 chapters:** 85 high-severity findings (34 chapters). Precision unmeasured.
- **Gate in report-only mode, 96 exports:** 2 clear, 62 need a look (rewrites never re-checked), 32 would be held (30 media, 6 open script flags).
- **Known-failure set:** 13 past failures with chapter and date are listed in the quality audit notes (lion-dance costumes narrated as monsters, hero named "Yu Shin", "C-Cursed" voiced "see, Cursed", missing explosion panel, wrong chapter's images, stale merge boards, thumbnail badge). They are not yet committed as regression fixtures.

### A/B tests run for the cost changes (recorded, $0.22)

| Change | Sample | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Describe at low thinking | 24 panels, Mount Hua 186 | text read from the panel identical on 24 of 24; thinking 269 → 0 tokens a panel; speaker labels differed from stored on 10 panels |
| Describe at default, second run | same 24 panels | speaker labels differed from stored on the same number (10) — so the difference above is normal variation |
| Crop planner, thinking budget 0 | 12 boxes | mean overlap with stored boxes 0.81, all ≥ 0.5 (default re-run: 0.86, 5 of 6); thinking ~1,880 → 0 tokens a call |

### What the benchmark cannot say yet

There is no human-labelled ground truth for fidelity, speakers or image placement, so no claim of
90% or 98–100% quality can be made. The scorecard gives the automated side; calibration needs a
person to label a sample (suggested: 5 chapters, every unit marked right/wrong, every placement
marked right/wrong) so the editor's and validator's precision and recall can be computed.

- **Projected after the branch:** median **$0.73** (range $0.52–$1.16); 25 of 26 chapters under $1, none under $0.50. Script thinking is kept. The re-check adds about $0.01.
- **To reach $0.50:** fold the subject box into the describe call (about −110 calls, −$0.18), then the floor is voice ($0.13) plus image input tokens. Flex or batch pricing would halve the model part if Google serves it.
- **Daily:** 4 new chapters a day ≈ $2.90 projected ($4.96 today), inside the $5–$7 target. The cap is $7.50 a day; autopilot's own budget is $6.
- **Failed attempts** are inside each chapter's figure. Calls that fail before a response are not logged; whether Google bills them is unknown.
- **Reused chapters** in a merged video cost nothing and are not counted as new output. A merge costs CPU only (212 s for 2 hours of video with the new join).
- **Fixed costs not measured here:** Railway bill, Upload-Post plan, Vercel. The Railway invoice was not available to this audit; the $10/month target is unverified.

Limits of these fixes:

- The spending hold uses the same per-call estimate as before. A call that costs more than its estimate can still take the day slightly past the cap; what is closed is the case of several calls each passing the check at once.
- A failed call is charged only when there is evidence the provider answered (tokens reported or parsed). A response lost in transit after the provider billed it is not visible to the studio.
- Upload-Post's answer for an id it has never seen is assumed to be HTTP 404, `not_found`, or an answer with no status. This was not checked against the live service (no upload was sent).
- `script_sha256` covers `script.txt`. Lines edited by hand on the board are stored with the board, not in `script.txt`, and are not covered by it.
- Merges built before this change have no stored evidence. They are checked from the chapter folders that still exist; a missing one is reported as `component_missing`.
- Series thumbnails are still re-rendered for every video of the series when defaults are applied to all; only the text details are protected.
- Cumulative mode changes what auto-append builds as soon as it is deployed: series with a new rendered chapter after their last posted video get a new, longer merge in Needs review. Builds cost no AI money and nothing posts without approval. `AUTO_APPEND_MODE=fresh` restores the previous rule.

### 12.3 Upload recovery

Provider behaviour was taken from Upload-Post's documentation (`/api/upload-video`, `/api/upload-status`, read 2026-11-14), not assumed: the client may supply `request_id`; an `Idempotency-Key` returns the existing job; an unknown id answers HTTP 404 with `status: not_found`; statuses are pending / queued / processing / in_progress / completed / failed, with a per-platform `success`. No upload was sent to observe this.

| Requirement | Status |
|---|---|
| Only a recognised, definitive rejection retires an id | fixed: a documented error answer (400/401/403/404/429 with JSON `success: false`) or an answer that says `success: false` |
| Empty answers, invalid JSON, missing fields, timeouts, connection failures, unavailable status → unknown | fixed (`classify_response`, `rejected`, `upload_detail`). The reviewed line `sure_no = not (created or {}).get("success")` is gone |
| Unknown keeps the id and requires reconciliation | fixed: every attempt is written to `publishes.json` before sending; the next attempt asks Upload-Post about each unsettled attempt and sends nothing while it cannot answer |
| Absent upload retried with the same id | fixed, only on the documented `not_found` |
| Partial success never duplicates the channel that succeeded | fixed: per-channel reconciliation; only channels that provably failed are sent again, under a new id |
| Success without a tracking id is not "published" | fixed: tracked by the studio's own id as "submitted" |
| Published / scheduled / accepted / processing kept apart | `submitted`, `published` (+ `visibility: scheduled`, `public_at`), `failed`, `unconfirmed` |

Tests (`test_durable_upload.py`, in-memory provider that follows the documentation): crash before sending, crash after acceptance before the result was saved, seven malformed answers, seven timeout / server-error shapes, status check down, documented refusal, partial success, partial success then crash, channel still processing, no tracking id, scheduled, synchronous per-platform answer. Not verified against the live service.

### 12.4 Spending

Measured on the production log (metered calls, 2026-10-20 to 11-13):

| Model | Calls | Held before (est.) | Median | p95 | p99 | Max | Calls above the old hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemini-3.8-flash | 9,465 | $0.005 | $0.0026 | $0.0118 | $0.0208 | $0.102 | 17% |
| gemini-2.5-flash (crop planner) | 1,509 | $0.001 | $0.0040 | $0.0081 | $0.0107 | $0.158 | 100% |
| gemini-3.5-flash | 451 | $0.012 | $0.0037 | $0.0272 | $0.0330 | $0.034 | 18% |

So the earlier "slightly" was wrong: one call cost 158 times what was held for it. Now:

- Holds are the measured 99th percentile, or the dearest of the last 200 real calls of that kind / model / stage if higher.
- Confirmed (metered), estimated (succeeded without usage), uncertain (no answer) and held money are separate and all count against the cap.
- A provider error answer (HTTP 4xx/5xx) is not charged. No answer at all (timeout, dropped connection, unknown error) is **uncertain** at the amount held. After 6 unanswered calls in a row a job is refused for 15 minutes.
- Holds of dead processes (30 min) and every hold present at server start become uncertain spend with a ledger line.
- Midnight: the day's buckets fold into lifetime; holds, learned sizes and unwritten ledger lines carry over.
- Ledger lines are saved with the money before they are appended; a failed append is retried on a later call (deduplicated by id). If `counters.json` cannot be saved, paid calls stop.
- Crop planner answers are capped at 8,192 output tokens. Describe and script caps exist (`VISION_/SCRIPT_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS`) but are off: the Interactions API field was not tried live.

Remaining exposure, stated plainly: a hold is a statistical bound, not a hard one. Until a stage has seen an expensive call, each concurrent call of that stage can exceed its hold by up to (its real cost − $0.021 for 3.8-flash); with the dearest call on record that is $0.081 per call. Describe and script calls have no hard output limit until their caps are switched on. Uncertain spend is an estimate of what may have been billed, not a measurement; the provider's bill is the only confirmation. A response the provider billed and the studio never received is booked as uncertain at the held amount, which can be lower than its real cost.

Tests: `test_spend_reservation.py` (22 tests: concurrent workers with mixed outcomes, restart recovery, crash mid-call, rollover, ledger and counter write failures), local fakes only.

### 12.5 Packaging lock

Approved, queued, being-posted, scheduled and posted exports are changed only by a person acting on that video. The guard sits where details are stored (`save_publish`) and in the thumbnail paths, so every automatic writer is covered: series defaults, automatic SEO, the chapter package, start-up title repair, tag trimming, bulk thumbnail redraws, the series look. An automatic step may still fill a field that is empty (late SEO); that is recorded. The thumbnail the upload will carry (series master + badge where the series has one) is worked out and fixed at approval, so nothing is substituted afterwards; approvals from before this change keep the old upload-time substitution, which is now written to the history. Retroactive series defaults need a preview and a list of named videos. Every change and every stopped change is in `<chapter>/packaging_revisions.jsonl`.

Limit: the approval-time thumbnail pin calls Drive during approval; it was tested with a stand-in, not against Drive.

### 12.6 Cumulative releases — what is proven, and how

`test_cumulative_e2e.py` drives the server's own build path with real ffmpeg on generated clips (each chapter has its own tone; the closing card is red with a 2 kHz tone):

- posted 1–4 + new chapter 5 → 1–5; length = old video + new chapter − the old video's closing card (within 0.15 s);
- the posted video and the chapter are byte-identical afterwards and no paid call is made;
- markers 1…5 in order at each story start (within 0.1 s); every chapter heard once, in order; no card left inside; the last card kept; decode clean; sound as long as the picture;
- title, tags, description and range carried over and relabelled;
- chain 1–4 → 1–5 → 1–6 with all earlier chapter folders deleted; chapters finishing out of order wait for the gap; four simultaneous requests build once; an approved release is not rebuilt over and not replaced when a later chapter arrives; the posted-chapter guard stops re-rendering and does not stop reuse; evidence carries the hash of every piece.

This test found a real defect: final-media QA of a compilation failed every line of a cleaned-up chapter as "narration-broken, critical" because it re-measures the chapters' original voice files. Fixed: for a compilation those lines are counted and named, and the compilation is judged on its own file.

Not proven: a cumulative build from real production single-chapter exports (none are on the volume or on this machine). The join itself ran on one real 62-minute production compilation on 2026-11-13.
